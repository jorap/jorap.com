meta_title: "Open Source - Permission, Not Purity"
description: "Open source means install without asking, read the code, and move your files - not a purity badge."
key_concept: |
  - Open source buys permission before it buys ideology.
  - Level 1: No license fee gets you in the door - freedom to install, break things locally, and read the source when docs lie is what keeps the stack.
  - Level 2: Open source is like owning the toolbox, not renting the workshop - you can open every drawer, copy the files, and leave when the landlord changes the rules.
  - Level 3: Because the license grants inspect and fork rights, the real win is moving your work when a host dies or a vendor pivots - not stickers on a laptop.
  - Level 4: Pick open tools when you need to learn on a student budget, hand clients something they keep, or survive a hosting disaster - still say no to abandonware and nulled plugins.
  - Level 5: [[Freedom Over Free]] names the split; [[Inspectable Stack]] is the filter; [[Community Depth]] picks which open project earns the evenings.
  - [[Future-Proofing Knowledge]] and [[Free Tier Hosting Stack]] are how open formats show up in my publish lane.
  - [[Git-Based CMS]] widens who can edit without forking the repo into a second database.
  - Open does not mean maintained, good, or free of labor - I still pay in evenings and say no to cracked themes.
examples:
  - "ASP examples lived behind licenses on a student budget - PHP and XAMPP portable let me unzip on a locked-down lab PC and actually run a CMS that weekend."
  - "Old PHP hosting died and posts were trapped - Hugo, markdown in git, and an MIT theme meant I could point the repo somewhere else without praying for an export plugin."
