Some weeknights I want the energy of those old ground-beef-and-pasta box meals - salty, soft, gone before anyone asks what's for dinner - without opening a packet of mystery powder. Glad I moved that job to the Instant Pot: **1 kg** lean beef, **1 kg** short pasta, one pressure cycle in my **8-quart**.

Short twisted pasta, taco seasoning without thickeners, **mushrooms** on top. Feeds 10-12, or dinner plus leftovers that reheat with a splash of broth.

The layering matters more than the spice packet. I used to stir meat, pasta, and tomato paste together before sealing - Instant Pot answered with a burn notice. What works: liquid under the noodles, meat and thick sauce on top, no stirring until after pressure.

## Why this batch size

The smaller version I started from was built for about 450 g of beef, less dried pasta, and bell peppers. That disappears at our table before I sit down. This batch is **1 kg beef** and **1 kg pasta** - about a 1:1 ratio - and it only fits an **8-quart Instant Pot** if you stay under the max-fill line. A 6-quart will not take this; cut the whole recipe in half for a smaller pot.

I brown the meat in a separate pan - same rule as my Instant Pot adobo. The Instant Pot stays clean: pasta first, then liquid, then beef on top. No fond to scrape, so no deglaze step. Extra dirty skillet is the tax I pay to skip the burn sensor fight.

Layer, do not stir. Pasta in the pot. Seasoned cooking liquid poured over. Reserved meat on top. Tomato paste sauce on top of the meat. Mushrooms last. Seal. Stir only after release.

Cooking liquid stays modest: about **1.65 L** broth for **1 kg** pasta - enough to cook the noodles without turning the pot into soup. Paste stays in a separate sauce layer on top. Do not whisk it into all the broth before it hits the pot. And skip taco seasoning with cornstarch - thickeners are another fast path to a burn notice. Spices only, not a gravy mix.

I use 95% lean so there is less grease to drain after the skillet. Fattier mince works if you drain it before it goes back on the pasta. Mushrooms replace the bell peppers - thick-sliced button or cremini, piled on top so they steam instead of turning to paste.

Timing is exact. High pressure **5 minutes**, then **exactly 5 minutes** natural release, then vent the rest. Full quick release makes the pasta stickier and the sauce wetter than I want. Soft noodles usually mean I waited longer than 5 minutes on natural release, or the box time was already short.

Active time is about **20 minutes** with the separate skillet. Total is about **45 minutes**, including pressure build and the timed release - a full kilo of pasta takes longer to come to pressure.

## My 1 kg Instant Pot batch

### Ingredients

#### Protein and aromatics

- 30 ml olive oil, about 2 tbsp
- 1 kg 95% lean ground beef, about 2.2 lbs
- 2 medium yellow onions, chopped
- about 100 g taco seasoning with no cornstarch or other thickeners, about two and a half to three 37 g / 1.3-ounce packets

#### Pasta, veg, and sauce

- 1 kg dried short pasta such as campanelle, gigli, penne, ziti, fusilli, or rigatoni, about 2.2 lbs
- 1.65 L chicken broth, about 7 cups (cooking liquid for 1 kg pasta; water with chicken powder works too)
- 170-180 ml tomato paste, about 2/3 cup + 1 tbsp to 3/4 cup
- 650 g mushrooms, thick-sliced, about 1.4 lbs
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 250 g grated Parmesan cheese, about 2 1/2 cups

### Notes before you start

Keep the **cooking liquid** and the **tomato paste sauce** separate until after pressure.

The **1.65 L chicken broth** is the full cooking liquid. No boxed broth on hand: use **1.65 L water** plus chicken powder to taste (about **4 tsp** if your powder is the usual strength, then adjust). Hold back **120 ml (about 1/2 cup)** of the broth to loosen the tomato paste into a pourable sauce. Pour the rest - **about 1.53 L (about 6 1/2 cups)** - over the pasta with most of the taco seasoning.

Broth plus taco seasoning already runs salty. I skip table salt and finish with **Parmesan** instead - that is the salt for this pot.
Reserve **2 tbsp of the taco seasoning** for the skillet with the beef and onion. The rest goes into the pasta cooking liquid.

Short pasta only here. Spaghetti needs the break-and-crisscross trick; campanelle and penne just need an even layer and enough liquid poured over.

If your pasta box says 10 minutes to al dente, half of that is 5 - that is the pressure number this batch uses. Much longer box times may need a minute more; angel hair and other thin noodles are the wrong shape for this timing.

At **1 kg** dried pasta, the inner pot looks full before you seal. If the food sits above the max-fill line, pull pasta out or split the batch. Do not force it.

## How I run it

### Step 1: Brown the beef and onion in a skillet

Put **30 ml olive oil (about 2 tbsp)** in a large skillet or wok over medium heat. When the oil is hot, add **1 kg 95% lean ground beef**, **2 medium yellow onions, chopped**, and **2 tbsp taco seasoning**. Cook, breaking the meat into 1/2-inch chunks and stirring often, until the onion is tender and the beef is mostly browned, **8-10 minutes**. The beef will finish under pressure. Drain any grease. Reserve the meat mixture.

Do not brown the meat in the Instant Pot for this recipe.

### Step 2: Layer the pasta and cooking liquid

Put the clean inner pot in the Instant Pot. Add **1 kg dried short pasta** in an even layer. Do not pack it down.

In a large measuring cup, stir the remaining taco seasoning (about **85 g**, or whatever is left from the **100 g** total) into **about 1.53 L chicken broth (about 6 1/2 cups)** - the **1.65 L** minus **120 ml (about 1/2 cup)** held back for the tomato paste sauce.

Pour that seasoned cooking liquid over the pasta. Gently press the pasta down with a spoon so most of it sits in the liquid. It is fine if a few pieces stick up. Do not add the tomato paste or the meat yet.

### Step 3: Top with meat, sauce, and mushrooms

Spoon the reserved beef and onion mixture over the pasta. Do not stir.

In a small bowl, whisk **170-180 ml tomato paste (about 2/3 cup + 1 tbsp to 3/4 cup)** with the reserved **120 ml chicken broth (about 1/2 cup)** until smooth and pourable. Pour this sauce over the meat. Do not stir. Thick sauce stays off the bottom of the pot.

Place **650 g thick-sliced mushrooms** on top. Do not stir.

### Step 4: Pressure cook

Lock the lid. Set the steam valve to **Sealing**. Select **Pressure Cook** on **High** pressure for **5 minutes**.

When the cooking time is up, leave the pot alone for **exactly 5 minutes** of natural release. Then turn the valve to **Venting** to release the remaining pressure.

### Step 5: Toss and finish

Open the lid. Gently toss the pasta, meat, sauce, and mushrooms together with a rubber spatula or tongs, scraping the bottom. The pasta will drink the last of the liquid as you stir. If it still looks soupy, put the lid back on for 1-2 minutes off heat, then toss again.

Season with freshly ground black pepper. Do not add table salt.

Toss or sprinkle with **250 g grated Parmesan (about 2 1/2 cups)** - that is the salt finish.

## What I'd tell you after the second pot

- Stay under the Instant Pot max-fill line. This **1 kg pasta** batch is packed even in an **8-quart**.
- Burn notice almost always means thick sauce under the pasta. Next time: pasta first, cooking liquid next, meat and tomato paste sauce on top only, no pre-stir.
- Stick to **1.65 L** cooking liquid for **1 kg** pasta. Soft or soupy pasta usually means the natural release ran past **exactly 5 minutes**, not that you needed more broth.
- Leftovers firm up in the fridge. A splash of broth or water in the pan when reheating brings the sauce back.

Glad this is the Instant Pot weeknight pasta I reach for when I want taco night without assembling a dozen soft shells. Same pot, one pressure cycle, layering that does not fight the burn sensor.
