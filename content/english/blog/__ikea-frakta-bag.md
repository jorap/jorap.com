Every few months another tote goes viral. Right now it's the Trader Joe's bag - limited drops, resale chatter, people treating a grocery sack like a collectible. Fine if that's your hobby. I already have a bag that does the job without the hunt.

Glad I never waited for a drop. The blue **medium FRAKTA** sits by the door, with a **BRATTBY** nested inside. When I leave the house with one hand free and errands ahead, that's what I grab.

---

## Trader Joe's is Aldi with better packaging

Here's the part a lot of the tote discourse skips: **Trader Joe's is Aldi**. Same German hard-discount family tree. Aldi Nord owns Trader Joe's. The cute Hawaiian shirts and limited tote drops sit on top of the same no-frills grocery machine that made Aldi famous - private label, tight assortments, low drama.

In the Philippines we already know that shape of store. **Dali** and **O!Save** are the Pinoy hard-discount cousins of the Aldi idea - bare aisles, cheap staples, no theater. Not the same ownership as Trader Joe's. Same instinct: cut the fluff, keep the price, get you in and out.

So when a Trader Joe's tote goes viral, I don't see a fashion moment. I see a hard-discount sack that learned how to do scarcity marketing. Cute branding on top of Aldi bones. I'll keep my blue FRAKTA and leave the drop culture to people who enjoy the chase.

## How the blue bag got here

FRAKTA wasn't born as an internet meme. It started as a store problem.

In the 1960s IKEA was selling more small stuff - tealights, glasses, cuttlery - and customers were juggling plastic baskets that couldn't hold the haul. **Ingvar Kamprad**, IKEA's founder, wanted a bag you could shoulder, hang on a trolley, or carry by hand - big enough for wallpaper rolls, tough enough for about 50 kilos.

On a trip to Taiwan, Kamprad and **Lars Göran Peterson** (IKEA's head of purchasing, often called LGP inside the company) met a factory owner who showed them polypropylene - the same stuff used for large rice bags. They liked it. Then a blunt strength check: someone who weighed about 50 kilos stood in a sample bag while Kamprad and Peterson lifted the handles. It held.

Yes - the finished bag still looks like a **heavy rice sack dyed IKEA blue**. Woven, slightly rustly, built to take abuse. Not canvas cosplay. Freight fabric.

First came yellow bags for in-store use and for sale. That mixed poorly at checkout - nobody could tell which bags were paid for. So IKEA split the colors. Yellow stays in the store. Blue goes home. That blue bag is **FRAKTA** - Swedish for carrying freight, which is exactly what it does.

Decades later the bag still looks like a tool. That's the point. It was never supposed to be a limited drop. It was supposed to solve the walk from shelf to car.

## BRATTBY, medium, large - pick the job

I only care about three bags in this blue family:

1. **BRATTBY** - the small square cousin. Same tough polypropylene as FRAKTA, scaled down for light days. 27×27×18.5 cm (10 5/8×10 5/8×7 1/4"), about 13 l (3.5 gallons), rated to 12 kg (26 lb).
2. **Medium FRAKTA** - my usual door bag. Soft rectangle, short and long handles, enough for a real errand without looking like I'm moving apartments. 45×18×45 cm / 36 l (17 3/4×7×17 3/4" / 10 gallons).
3. **Large FRAKTA** - the classic big tote. This is my shopping-trolley bag, and the same one that swallows multiple badminton rackets. 55×37×35 cm / 71 l (21 3/4×14 1/2×13 3/4" / 19 gallons), rated to 25 kg (55 lb).

Capacity is a menu, not a personality. I almost chased a nicer-looking tote once. Then I looked at what I already owned and realized the blue stack already won on volume and abuse. Skip the upgrade when the boring bag still has room.

## What actually lives in mine

Not a fashion prop. A kit.

Usual carry: **medium FRAKTA**, with a **BRATTBY** nested inside, plus **an umbrella** and a **small fan**. Rain hits, fan comes out in a hot wait, and the small bag is already there if the haul splits or someone needs a spare.

Light day flip: I grab the **BRATTBY** and fold **two medium FRAKTAs** inside it. Small bag on the shoulder. Two empties ready if the stop turns into a bigger shop. You don't dig for plastic. You don't borrow. You already packed the upgrades.

Big haul / court day: the **large FRAKTA** hangs on the shopping trolley, or it eats a few badminton rackets without me playing bag tetris. Same blue sack. Different job.

A slim viral tote looks sharp empty. This stack still works when you stuff real weight into it - and when you need a second bag without walking back to the car.

## That manly IKEA blue

I'm not shopping for a muted canvas flex. I want the bag that says tool, not accessory.

The FRAKTA's **IKEA blue** does that for me. Bright enough to spot in a pile. Plain enough that nobody mistakes it for a limited drop. Manly in the way a wrench is manly - useful first, status never.

If your tote needs a story about how hard it was to find, you're collecting. If your bag is blue, slightly scuffed, and still opening wide for another load, you're carrying. I know which one I want by the door.

People can keep the soft neutrals and the logo chase. I'll keep the blue that reads as errand gear from across the parking lot.

---

## Hype is not a feature

Viral tote culture sells scarcity. The FRAKTA sells the opposite: it's in the IKEA aisle next to the tape and the plant pots. You walk in for something else, you walk out with a bag that outlives the trend cycle.

Trader Joe's can keep the Hawaiian shirts and the tote drops. Aldi energy with a cult brand on top is still Aldi energy. Here at home, Dali and O!Save already taught that lesson without the collectible sack.

I'm glad the loud tote wave exists in one sense - it makes the FRAKTA look even more honest. No waitlist. No "did you get one?" No pretending a sack is a personality. Just a BRATTBY, medium and large FRAKTA, and the blue that doesn't apologize.

Boring gear earns its keep by showing up. This one shows up wet, hot, overloaded, and still ready for the next load. That's the whole review.

Grab the FRAKTA. Pack what you actually need. Leave the viral tote for people who want the chase.
