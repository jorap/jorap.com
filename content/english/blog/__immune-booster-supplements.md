**Disclaimer:** I'm not a doctor. This isn't medical advice. Talk to a clinician before starting supplements - especially if you take medication, are pregnant, or have chronic conditions. This is my personal notebook, not your prescription.

---

## Why I bother at all

Last rainy season I caught three colds in eight weeks - jeepney commute, school pickup, one kid bringing home whatever went around. I can't sterilize the city. Glad I can still control **sleep, food, movement, and a modest supplement stack** when baseline habits slip.

I almost bought a "complete immune" bundle off a Shopee listing with twenty ingredients I couldn't pronounce. Put it back. If I can't name why each bottle is there, it's shopping, not a stack.

Supplements don't replace vaccines, hand washing, or rest. They're **margin**, not magic.

---

## D3, zinc, C, and elderberry when everyone's sniffling

**Vitamin D3** - I take it with a meal that has fat. Lab numbers matter more than guessing; I don't mega-dose blind. I skipped labs for a stretch once and realized I'd been guessing with confidence. Not the same as knowing.

**Zinc (picolinate)** - one form I tolerate. I don't run high zinc long-term without guidance. Took zinc on an empty stomach once - lesson learned, meal first.

**Vitamin C** - food first. I bump a modest supplement during high-exposure weeks, split doses, not one heroic scoop. The giant scoop version sat in the pantry and felt like progress. It wasn't better than the modest split.

**Elderberry** - early-season support when everyone around me gets sniffly. Not a cure. I treat it as "maybe shortens the misery" not "prevents."

**Quercetin / NAC** - optional, mixed evidence. I don't build the whole stack on them. They're the bottles I forget first when life gets loud - which tells me they're optional.

---

## Daily vs seasonal

**Baseline when I'm consistent:** D3, moderate zinc, food + modest C.

**Exposure bump:** elderberry when colds spread, slightly more sleep vigilance (honestly the biggest lever), NAC if respiratory bugs are in the house.

Commute season is when the stack matters most - and when I'm most likely to skip sleep and call the pills the plan. That's backwards. The pills are backup for the weeks food and rest slip, not a license to skip both.

Rainy season last year I ran on six hours and three supplements and still caught whatever Isaiah brought home from school. The week I fixed sleep first, the colds spaced out even without changing the bottles. Pills didn't fail. Sleep did.

---

## What actually works better

Seven hours of sleep beats seven supplements. Water and real meals beat gummies. Walks count. And cortisol isn't a meme - the weeks I'm stressed are the weeks I get sick, supplements or not.

I still get sick. The stack doesn't make me invincible. It makes me feel like I did the boring baseline when life gets loud.

---

Monday: fix sleep before you buy bottles. If you still want a stack after that, ask your clinician which two supplements matter - not the twenty-ingredient Shopee bundle.

I'm not optimizing for invincibility. Glad I'm stacking boring margin on boring habits - sleep, food, wash hands - and reaching for a bottle when the jeepney season wins anyway.
