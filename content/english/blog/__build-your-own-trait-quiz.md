I love preference quizzes. Still do - glad that itch never fully went away. (I grew up calling them personality tests. Same itch. Different word for what I actually mean now.)

The first one I remember was the old four-type chart - sanguine, choleric, melancholy and phlegmatic. Fun at a youth group. Then it wore thin. Everyone got shoved into a mood board. Sixteen types felt sharper. More room to say "this is how I prefer to work" without pretending four boxes covered a whole person.

Still incomplete. Two people can share the same sixteen-stack and fight the moment the clock, the chat, or the outage hits. So I add a fifth switch - **Urgency** (Wait / Go). Math goes from sixteen calm stacks to **thirty-two** under fire.

A book pushed me further: *Please Understand Me*. That was the spark. Later I learned something that matters for builders: a lot of the **ideas** behind type talk sit in a long public conversation. Jung, temperaments, preference language people have argued about for decades. What does **not** sit free for you to copy is a branded assessment's wording, item bank, official labels, and marketing dress.

So the claim is simple. **Don't clone the famous quiz.** Build your own preference quiz. Name it something else. Add the piece you actually want to own - for me, Urgency.

I'm not a lawyer. This is product hygiene and naming honesty - the same instinct that keeps me away from nulled themes and fake "premium" plugins.

---

## The clone trap

The clone looks harmless. A free "what type are you" widget. Same four preference pairs. Same letter codes. Questions that feel suspiciously close to the official set. Result blurbs that read like a paraphrase of someone else's type pages.

That isn't a tribute. That's borrowing someone else's product surface.

What you can often work with, carefully and in your own words: the broad idea that people differ in preference - energy, information, decisions, lifestyle. Public-domain and public-conversation stuff. What you should treat as off-limits: their questions, their trademarked names, their lookalike scoring copy, and result text that is just a rewrite of theirs.

Open source taught me a blunt rule. **Name what is yours.** License what you share. Don't pretend a fork of a closed product is a clean start. A preference quiz deserves the same honesty.

## Five switches, thirty-two stacks

Here's the part people mean when they say "sixteen types." Four preference questions about calm style. Each has two sides. Sixteen stacks fall out of the math. I add a fifth - **Urgency** - and each stack splits into Wait or Go. That is **thirty-two**.

**Rule for all five switches:** they are **preferences**, not personality. **There is no best answer** on either side of any switch - energy, info, decide, plan, or urgency. You lean one way. The other way still works. Neither pole gets a gold star.

The **idea** of preference pairs has floated around psychology and popular books for a long time. The **wording below is mine** - sketches so you can see the map, not a paste of anyone's manual, and not a free substitute for a branded instrument.

If you ship a product later, rename these. Letter codes are common shorthand in conversation. They are a bad product name if your quiz is trying to look like someone else's box.

### Outward energy vs inward energy

Some people refill by being with others - talk, rooms, back-and-forth. Some refill alone - quiet, one conversation, time to think before they speak. Same person can do both. Preference is which one costs less. No best side.

### Facts in front of you vs patterns in your head

Some trust what they can point at - steps, details, what already worked. Some trust the leap - hunches, "what if," the shape of the problem before the checklist. Both can be wrong. Preference is where attention goes first. No best side.

### Decide by head vs decide by heart

Some sort a choice by logic, fairness as a rule, "does this make sense on paper." Some sort by people impact, values in the room, "how does this land on someone I care about." Neither is colder or warmer as a human. Preference is which filter you reach for when the call is hard. No best side.

### Plan it vs keep it open

Some want a settled path - lists, deadlines, closed loops. Some want options alive - room to pivot, late decisions, "we'll know when we get there." Preference shows up in how you pack a bag and how you hate a sudden calendar change. No best side.

### Urgency - Wait vs Go

The four above answer how you prefer to run on an easy Tuesday. **Urgency** asks what tempo you prefer when Tuesday catches fire - clock, angry chat, site down, someone yelling you're late.

- **Wait (W)** - Buy a beat. Slow the moment. Take the tempo back.
- **Go (G)** - Move now. Push through. Let urgency take the first step.

Same rule: **preference, no best answer.** Wait without a next step becomes freeze. Go without a check becomes a mess. Both are useful on a team. Not the same as plan-vs-open. Not grit. Not who volunteers first. Only: under fire, which tempo do you prefer - pause or push?

Cross the four calm switches → sixteen base stacks. Add Urgency → **thirty-two** results (each base name + W or G). Below: name table for the sixteen bases, then plain sketches, then the full thirty-two list.

## The sixteen bases, in my own words

Each base is a **calm preference** first, then how that same lean shifts under **Urgency** - Wait vs Go. Written for any team - work, school, sports, volunteers, family project. Grouped by the middle pair so the list stays readable. The W and G lines are preference under load, not a better/worse grade.

Working titles below are **mine**. Letter codes stay as map shorthand. Middle-column nicknames stay out of the product.

### Sensing + Thinking - practical and blunt

**Rule Keeper (ISTJ)** (inward, facts, head, plan). Quiet keeper of how the team is supposed to run. Remembers the rule, the checklist, the last time this went wrong. Steady and exact when the week is calm.  
- **Rule Keeper-W:** Slows the scramble - "check the rule, then move." Buys time so nobody skips the step that usually saves you.  
- **Rule Keeper-G:** Corrects mid-chaos out loud. Restores order fast, even if people feel pushed.

**Quiet Helper (ISFJ)** (inward, facts, heart, plan). Notices what teammates need before they ask. Loyal on the small tasks. Does the unglamorous follow-through.  
- **Quiet Helper-W:** Pulls one person aside, asks what they need, then helps. Care with a pause.  
- **Quiet Helper-G:** Jumps in to fix everyone's problem at once until empty.

**Fast Fixer (ESTP)** (outward, facts, head, open). Solves what is in front of the team right now. Reads the room fast, moves, adjusts. Prefers a real snag over a long theory talk.  
- **Fast Fixer-W:** One breath to see the mess, then the smallest useful fix. Still hates long meetings - just won't thrash blind.  
- **Fast Fixer-G:** Hands on the problem while others are still naming it. Talks over the plan because waiting already feels late.

**Plan Closer (ESTJ)** (outward, facts, head, plan). Organizes people and deadlines out loud. Wants clear owners and done dates. Good at making a messy group finish.  
- **Plan Closer-W:** Sets a later lock time - "decide by three, names on the board." Gets tempo back.  
- **Plan Closer-G:** Hands out jobs in the hallway now. Can turn into the enforcer when the team needed flexibility.

### Sensing + Feeling - practical and people-first

**Solo Fixer (ISTP)** (inward, facts, head, open). Quiet hands-on fixer. Keeps a lot inside. Learns by taking the problem apart. Helps with action more than speeches.  
- **Solo Fixer-W:** Steps away, figures it out alone, comes back with a fix. Skips the circle-up on purpose.  
- **Solo Fixer-G:** Same quiet exit - but starts tearing into the problem before the team agrees what broke.

**Soft Heart (ISFP)** (inward, facts, heart, open). Gentle in the group. Strong personal taste. Notices tone and whether something feels off. Shows care in small concrete ways.  
- **Soft Heart-W:** Goes quiet, softens voice, asks one careful question before jumping in.  
- **Soft Heart-G:** Moves straight into comfort or a quick fix - without saying the hard thing out loud.

**Fun Starter (ESFP)** (outward, facts, heart, open). Brings energy into the team. Makes ordinary moments feel lighter. Reads people live and responds.  
- **Fun Starter-W:** Holds the joke. Lets the hard talk land first. Warm without covering the problem.  
- **Fun Starter-G:** Keeps everyone laughing mid-crisis. Can dodge the hard talk by entertaining through it.

**Group Glue (ESFJ)** (outward, facts, heart, plan). Holds the team together. Remembers roles, birthdays, who felt left out. Wants people working well with each other.  
- **Group Glue-W:** Calls a short pause - "breathe, then we go around." Harmony with a gate.  
- **Group Glue-G:** Smooths conflict in public right away - jumps between people before the facts settle.

### Intuition + Feeling - big picture and people

**Quiet Dreamer (INFJ)** (inward, patterns, heart, plan). Quiet long-view teammate. Sees how people and meaning fit together. Speaks less, weighs more.  
- **Quiet Dreamer-W:** Steps back, connects the dots privately, then speaks once. Slow, then clear.  
- **Quiet Dreamer-G:** Shares the big meaning too early - deep talk while the team still needs a next step.

**Heart Keeper (INFP)** (inward, patterns, heart, open). Guided by personal values. Needs the work to mean something. Protects what feels right.  
- **Heart Keeper-W:** Steps back until the choice matches the value. May look checked out.  
- **Heart Keeper-G:** Defends the value out loud mid-fight - principles first, logistics later.

**Idea Starter (ENFP)** (outward, patterns, heart, open). Sparks possibilities and people. Connects teammates and ideas in the same afternoon. Starts many threads.  
- **Idea Starter-W:** Holds the new idea, checks who it hits, then shares. Still creative - just gated.  
- **Idea Starter-G:** Throws new ideas into the urgent huddle. Fresh options when the team needed to finish one thing.

**Cheer Friend (ENFJ)** (outward, patterns, heart, plan). Sees who someone could become and talks them toward it. Watches team morale.  
- **Cheer Friend-W:** One steady pep talk after a short pause - morale with timing.  
- **Cheer Friend-G:** Coaches everyone at once under fire. Burns out carrying other people's panic.

### Intuition + Thinking - big picture and systems

**Quiet Planner (INTJ)** (inward, patterns, head, plan). Builds a picture of how the team should work, then tries to make reality match. Patient with strategy, impatient with busy fluff.  
- **Quiet Planner-W:** Buys a beat before changing the plan - old plan still available, then switch.  
- **Quiet Planner-G:** Starts changing the plan while still talking. The model wins; feelings can feel like noise.

**Why Asker (INTP)** (inward, patterns, head, open). Curious questioner. Asks the thing that stalls the meeting - usually for a good reason. Lives in how ideas fit.  
- **Why Asker-W:** Keeps asking until the problem is clear - decision waits on purpose.  
- **Why Asker-G:** Asks the stalling question out loud in the urgent huddle - can sound cold while still sorting.

**Idea Flipper (ENTP)** (outward, patterns, head, open). Plays with angles. Flips the problem, stress-tests the idea, finds what nobody listed. Bored by rote.  
- **Idea Flipper-W:** One good challenge, then pick. Debate with a stop rule.  
- **Idea Flipper-G:** Keeps arguing for fun of the angle while the finish line waits. Talk as motion.

**Hard Decider (ENTJ)** (outward, patterns, head, plan). Direct about results. Sets direction, assigns weight, wants movement. Comfortable with hard calls.  
- **Hard Decider-W:** Hard call with a short gate - "two minutes, then we lock."  
- **Hard Decider-G:** Pushes pace now - hesitation reads as not buying the goal. Movement is the message.

None of these paragraphs are quiz items. None are licensed type pages. They are **my** sketches of sixteen calm bases and how each splits under Urgency into thirty-two - for any team that hits a deadline together.

## What I'd build instead

I'd write my own dimensions in plain language. My own questions. My own result names - Rule Keeper-W, Idea Starter-G, and the rest of the thirty-two - with four-letter codes only as map shorthand, not as the product brand. I'd call it a **preference quiz** in the UI copy - not "discover your personality." If the quiz lives on a site I ship, the product name should sound like **my** tool, not a knockoff aisle.

I'd also keep the scope honest. This is a reflection quiz for curiosity or team talk. Not a hiring instrument. Not clinical diagnosis. Not "science-proven" marketing I can't defend. Not a personality sentence.

## The gap I want to own: Urgency

Most type talk I see stops at "how you prefer to be when things are fine." Fine. Incomplete. Sixteen calm stacks still leave two Quiet Planners fighting on deploy night.

**Urgency** is the proprietary fifth **preference** switch. Not "borrow their types and slap panic modes on top." My own questions about under-fire tempo. My own W/G result language. Still a preference - same family as the other four - not a personality stamp and not a winner between Wait and Go.

### Fifth letter: Urgency (Wait / Go)

| Letter | Pole | Plain meaning |
| ------ | ---- | ------------- |
| **W** | Wait | Buy a beat. Slow the moment. Take the tempo back. |
| **G** | Go | Move now. Push through. Let urgency take the first step. |

Full result = base name + Urgency letter (thirty-two total). **W** and **G** stay off the four-letter map on purpose (no clash with E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P).

This sits on [Pressure Reveals Weakness](/notes/pressure-reveals-weakness/) habits I already write about - slow the moment, control the rhythm - not on someone else's "assertive vs turbulent" dress.

### Ten sample Urgency items (draft fuel)

Raw scenes first. Live form will be **4-point Likert** (next section) - rewrite each scene as a single agree/disagree line. For a quick tally while drafting: pick the closer answer, count **W** vs **G**. Majority wins. Ties = "both show up; name which one costs you more when it is actually urgent."

1. Group chat blows up with a sharp accusation about you. First move?  
   **W** - One breath. Read twice. Answer the fact later.  
   **G** - Reply now before the story hardens.

2. Teacher (or boss) wants an answer "before you leave the room" on something messy.  
   **W** - "I'll send a clear answer by X." Buy time.  
   **G** - Give the best answer you have standing up.

3. Fire drill energy at home - keys, bag, someone yelling you're late.  
   **W** - Stop. Checklist. Then move.  
   **G** - Grab and go. Fix details in motion.

4. Teammate dumps a last-minute crisis in your lap.  
   **W** - Ask what "done" means and when it's actually due.  
   **G** - Start fixing while they are still talking.

5. You send the wrong file. Thread is live.  
   **W** - Pause, name the miss cleanly, then repair.  
   **G** - Flood the thread with fixes and follow-ups immediately.

6. Argument rising at the dinner table. Voices climbing.  
   **W** - Lower your voice. One clarifying question.  
   **G** - Match pace so your point lands before the room moves on.

7. Big test / launch tomorrow. Brain spinning at 11pm.  
   **W** - Stop. Sleep. Trust the prep you already did.  
   **G** - One more pass. Motion beats lying there anxious.

8. Someone cuts the line / cuts you off in traffic-level small injustice.  
   **W** - One breath. Don't become the version you hate.  
   **G** - Push back in the moment so it doesn't slide.

9. Plan just broke five minutes before the event.  
   **W** - Rewrite the smallest plan that still works.  
   **G** - Improvise live. Announce the new move while walking.

10. You are tired and still need one hard decision tonight.  
    **W** - Delay the call until morning if the stakes allow.  
    **G** - Decide now so tomorrow starts already moving.

These ten are **mine** - sample items for Urgency, not a paste from any branded instrument. If you ship a quiz, rewrite them again in your voice until they aren't copies of this post either.

That is the uniqueness. Public ideas for the four-letter floor. **Urgency (W/G)** turns sixteen into thirty-two.

## Questionnaire formats compared

Same trait. Different shells. Format choice changes what you measure and how fakeable the quiz feels.

| Format | How it looks | Good for | Weak spot | My take |
| ------ | ------------ | -------- | --------- | ------- |
| **Forced choice (A or B)** | Two moves; pick the closer one | Sharp binary push | Feels blunt when both are "a little me" | Solid backup. The ten Urgency scenes above started here. |
| **4-point Likert (no middle)** | Strongly disagree / disagree / agree / strongly agree - **four boxes only** | Preference strength without a hide-in-the-middle button | Still need Wait-worded *and* Go-worded items so "agree" isn't always the hero | **What I'll ship.** No neutral. You pick a side. |
| **5-point Likert (with middle)** | Same, plus "neither" / "not sure" | Soft research surveys | Middle becomes the escape hatch | Skip. I don't want a middle answer. |
| **Yes / No on one line** | "I buy time before I answer." Yes/No | Tiny mobile quizzes | No middle; wording bias wins | Same spirit as 4-point, less nuance. |
| **Scene + 3–4 options** | Short story; pick best move among several | Training, coaching debriefs | Scoring gets messy | Workshops yes. Letter engine no. |
| **Rank order** | Drag statements into "most like me" | Priority, not a single letter | Slow on phones | Skip for v1. |
| **Slider (0–100)** | Continuous Wait ↔ Go | Pretty UI | False precision | Skip until forced data says otherwise. |
| **Open text** | "What do you do when the chat explodes?" | Interviews, human scoring | Can't auto-type | Optional debrief after the score, not the letter engine. |

### Why 4-point Likert for mine

I want strength ("agree" vs "strongly agree") without a couch in the middle. Five-point quizzes teach people to tap 3 and leave. Four-point says: lean Wait or lean Go - **a preference lean, not the correct answer.**

How an Urgency item looks in that shell:

> When a group chat blows up with a sharp accusation about me, I buy a breath and answer the facts later.  
> 1 Strongly disagree · 2 Disagree · 3 Agree · 4 Strongly agree

Score Wait-leaning items toward **W**, reverse-score Go-leaning items (or write separate Go lines and score those toward **G**). Cut the letter at the midpoint of the possible total. The A/B scenes above are raw material - rewrite each as a single statement for the Likert form.

### How many items?

| Load | Rough size | Feels like | Risk |
| ---- | ---------- | ---------- | ---- |
| Tiny | 1–2 per axis (5–10 total for five letters) | Party quiz | One moody tap flips a letter |
| **Lean (my target)** | **4 per axis** (about **20** if all five letters; or **4–8** if Urgency is a bolt-on) | Short enough to finish on a phone | Still need both poles worded both ways |
| Standard hobby | 6–10 per axis (30–50 total) | "Real quiz" | Dropout climbs; fine for weekend curious |
| Long battery | 15+ per axis / 70+ total | Research cosplay | People quit; you maintain junk items |

**My pick:** **four items per axis** on the 4-point scale. For Urgency alone that's four Wait/Go statements (mix of Wait-worded and Go-worded). For a full five-letter quiz, twenty items is enough to ship a v1 without pretending I'm a psych lab.

Fewer than four per axis and a single bad day owns your result. More than ten per axis and I'm designing for dropout, not clarity.

### What I'd ship

- **4-point Likert, no middle** for every scored letter (including Urgency).
- **Four items per axis** to start - expand only if real answers look noisy.
- Forced-choice scenes stay in the notebook as draft fuel, not the live form.

## Practical use of Urgency (the thirty-two)

Four letters say how you prefer to work when the room is calm. **Urgency** says what tempo you prefer when the clock, the chat, or the room turns hot. Same base name, two results - that is why the map has thirty-two. Neither W nor G is the "right" teammate.

### Pair the letters on purpose

Same four-letter stack, different fifth preference - different Tuesday.

| Stack (example) | + Wait (W) | + Go (G) |
| --------------- | ---------- | -------- |
| Quiet Planner | Buys a beat before rewriting the plan | Starts changing production while talking |
| Idea Starter | Parks the spark, checks the blast radius | Launches the idea into the group chat mid-crisis |
| Plan Closer | Locks a later done-time | Assigns owners in the hallway now |
| Soft Heart | Softens voice, asks one question | Moves to fix feelings with immediate action |

The practical move is not "W is better" or "G is better." There is **no best answer**. It is **name the preference mismatch before the deadline**.

### Where I'd actually use it

**Client / freelance handoff.** Two people both score as Plan Closers. One is **Plan Closer-W**, one is **Plan Closer-G**. Without Urgency they keep accusing each other of "not caring about the deadline." With it: G owns the first response and the war-room energy; W owns the written plan and the "answer by 3pm" commit. Same base dress. Different jobs when it is urgent.

**Family morning rush.** Keys, bag, someone late. A **G** parent starts moving; a **W** parent wants the checklist. Fight is not about love - it is Urgency. Deal: G starts the car; W runs the two-line list at the door the night before so Wait already happened.

**Build / deploy night.** Site is broken. **G** wants the hotfix live. **W** wants one reproduce step and a rollback tab open. Pair them: G drives the patch branch; W keeps the previous deploy one click away ([change window](/notes/change-window/) thinking). Urgency makes the pair explicit instead of passive-aggressive Slack.

**Retro after a bad week.** Ask one question: "Under heat this sprint, did we Wait or Go too hard?" Not a personality roast - a process note. Too much G → missed rollback. Too much W → missed the window. Adjust next sprint's default.

### Rules of use (so it stays practical)

1. **Score Urgency only on load stories** - deadlines, fights, outages - not on favorite coffee order.
2. **Don't hire, fire, or rank people on W/G** - or on any of the five preferences. Reflection and pairing only.
3. **Say the quiet part in the result copy:** preferences, not personality; no best pole on Wait or Go.
4. **Publish the pair job** when two people collide: who buys time, who moves first - both jobs count.
5. **Re-test after a real incident** - one outage teaches more than one quiz night.

## Hard no / soft yes

**Hard no**

- Reusing official questions or close paraphrases
- Using their trademarked product names as your product name
- Shipping letter-code results that are meant to read as that brand
- Marketing "the same test, free"

**Soft yes**

- Your trait names and plain-English scales
- Your items, written from scratch - **4-point Likert, no middle**; about **four items per axis**
- Your five switches framed as **preferences, not personality** - including Urgency
- **No best answer** on Wait or Go (or on any other pole)
- Your fifth switch - **Urgency (Wait / Go)** - sixteen bases become thirty-two results
- Clear credits when you learned from a book (*Please Understand Me* pointed me here) without pretending you own that author's full system either

If you want to share the non-proprietary shell later - open items, open scoring notes - do it on purpose, with a license. Keep the Urgency engine behind the line you choose.

## What I'd tell a friend Monday

Don't hunt for a PDF of the famous quiz to "adapt." That path is the problem.

Sketch five **preference** switches in plain language. Write four ugly Likert items per axis - no middle box - in your own voice. Add Urgency (Wait / Go) so you ship thirty-two results, not sixteen. Put in the result page: no best W or G. Name the product like you'd name a board game retheme - clear it isn't someone else's box.

If the first version feels too generic, good. That's the old four-type chart problem again. Sharpen it. Don't steal sharpness from a brand.

The reader takeaway I want stuck in your head: **do not reuse their questions or labels.** Build yours. Own the gap you care about - for me, Urgency as a fifth preference that turns sixteen calm stacks into thirty-two under fire, with no winner between Wait and Go - and leave the clone aisle alone. Glad I built one that fits the gap I actually care about.
