The lesson hit me on rented PHP hosting, not on Facebook. Client left, bill stayed, files sat on a server I didn't really control. When that setup died, a chunk of my work went with it. Same gut punch people describe when a platform pivots or throttles reach - just smaller and personal. Glad that loss pushed me toward a landlord seat.

I still post on social. I still get the "why not just use Facebook?" question. Fair.

What helped wasn't another rant about algorithms. It was a **sorting frame** - three places your words can live, and a rule for which bucket gets the real copy. For why I bother with a site at all, see [why I still run my own website](/blog/why-run-your-own-website/). For the publishing habit around it, see [sustainable online publishing](/blog/sustainable-online-publishing/). This post is just the frame.

---

## Bucket one: on their servers

Facebook, X, YouTube, group chats, newsletter platforms you don't export from.

**Upside:** Fast start. Built-in audience. Low friction the day you have a thought.

**Downside:** Search inside a group is unreliable. Export is a mess when you need it. Terms shift. Reach throttles. Your best thread from 2019 isn't on your resume - it's behind their login.

I use this bucket for **distribution**. I do not use it as the only copy of anything I'd miss in five years.

---

## Bucket two: on rented hosting you pay for

Classic shared hosting. Managed WordPress. A database you never open.

**Upside:** Feels like a real site. Familiar admin panel. Monthly bill instead of learning Git.

**Downside:** Feels like ownership until the math stops working or the provider changes hands. Content can still trap in formats that only export if you pay or pray. I learned that when [my old PHP hosting died](/blog/how-i-built-jorap-notes/).

I still recommend WordPress for plenty of client jobs. I just don't confuse **renting a server** with **owning files**.

---

## Bucket three: in files you control

Domain you renew. Content in Git or plain markdown on disk. Build on push.

**Upside:** Clone the repo and you have everything. Point the domain elsewhere if the host changes terms. Boring formats outlive trendy dashboards.

**Downside:** You trade a hosting bill for Git pushes, broken YAML at 11pm, and the occasional "why did this build fail" evening. I lost one night to a bad indent once - Hugo's error pointed at the wrong line. Fixed it same night.

My bucket three today: Hugo, GitHub, Cloudflare Pages on the free tier - same shape as [how I built this site](/blog/how-i-built-jorap-notes/). No admin panel. No PHP cron jobs.

---

## The rule I actually follow

**Social is the megaphone. Bucket three is the vault.** Bucket two is fine when the job needs it - just know which bucket you're in.

Before I publish something I care about:

1. Does the **canonical version** live in bucket three (or a client repo they own)?
2. Is the social post only a **link and a teaser**, not the only copy?
3. If this app disappeared tomorrow, would I still have the text?

If the answer to three is no, I'm posting in the wrong bucket.
