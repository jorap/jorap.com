I'm not a physiotherapist. I sit for work. My back gets stiff after long WordPress build weeks where I skip lunch breaks and work from the couch with the laptop in my lap.

**Stuart McGill's "Big Three"** - curl-up, side plank, bird dog - is the low-back routine I keep **bookmarked**, not one I've turned into a daily habit yet. Simple. No gym. Three moves that don't grind the spine the way my old crunch habit did.

Not medical advice. If you're injured, see a professional. This is my reference page for stiff days - when I actually get off the couch and do the work.

---

## Bracing and endurance, not crunch reps

For years my "core work" was flexion-heavy - sit-ups, crunches, that ab-wheel clip I saved and never stuck with. My lower back felt worse after, not better. I'd finish a set, sit down at the laptop, and the stiff feeling came back before the commit finished.

McGill focuses on **bracing and endurance** - teaching your trunk to stabilize without curling your spine under load for reps. That's the part I needed. The trio trains the trunk in the neutral-ish positions I actually sit in all day.

I almost skipped the whole thing because it looked too small. Minimal movement. Short holds. No sweat selfie. Then I tried one honest circuit after a couch-coding afternoon and the next morning's standup felt less like a hinge needing oil.

---

## Curl-up (not a crunch)

**Goal:** train the front of the core **without** full spinal flexion reps.

I used to chase "elbows to knees." That's a crunch. The McGill curl-up is smaller: lie on your back, one knee bent, hands under the lower back so it keeps its natural curve. Lift head and shoulders a little - **stiffen and hover** - hold briefly, lower with control. Alternate which leg is bent between sets.

The first time I did it, I overshot. Chin to chest, shoulders too high, breath held like I was deadlifting. Felt like a crunch with extra steps. The demo that clicked for me was [McGill's curl-up](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vLpHJ1Cxj6k) - tiny range, honest tension.

If your neck screams, you went too far. Shrink the lift. The work is in the brace, not the travel.

---

## Side plank

**Goal:** lateral core and hip stability - the side that collapses when you sit crooked for three hours.

Forearm on the floor, body in a straight line. Start knees down if the full plank turns into a banana. Hold quality over ego duration. **10-20 seconds** done well beats a saggy minute.

I failed this one on pride. First week I chased a minute each side. Hips drooped, neck craned at the TV, form gone by twenty seconds. Reset: shorter holds, hips forward, neck neutral. Switch sides. Stop when the line breaks, not when the timer looks impressive.

[Side plank demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VAPN4CmUWqk) if you want a visual. Watch the hips - that's where I cheat.

---

## Bird dog

**Goal:** hold the trunk steady while opposite arm and leg reach. Anti-slouch in motion.

Hands and knees, spine neutral. Extend opposite arm and leg **without** rotating the hips or arching the low back. Slow reps. Pause at the end. Imagine a cup of water on your lower back.

My early bird dogs looked busy and wrong - arm and leg flailing, hips twisting toward the window, low back dipping like a hammock. The cup-of-water cue fixed more than any rep count. I still catch myself rushing on tired days. Slow is the point.

[Bird dog demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S1QbyYZaXIg) - watch for the still hips, not the high kick.

## How I actually run the circuit

When I do it, it's boring on purpose. Two or three rounds:

| Exercise | Reps / time |
|---|---|
| Curl-up | 5-8 per side |
| Side plank | 10-20 sec each side |
| Bird dog | 6-8 each side |

**Frequency:** three or four times a week on light days, or a micro-session when I've been desk-bound. Not daily yet - that's the honest gap. Bookmarking isn't the same as showing up.

**Breathing:** don't hold your breath like you're deadlifting a car. I still catch myself doing that on the side plank. Exhale on the effort. Sounds basic. I forget it weekly.

I keep the three YouTube demos in a phone folder labeled "back" next to the kitchen charger. When the couch laptop week hits, the barrier is opening the folder - not remembering the names.

---

Low back care doesn't need fancy equipment. **Curl-up, side plank, bird dog** - form over heroics, short holds over saggy minutes.

Bookmark the demos. Run the circuit. Sit down again with a slightly more honest spine. That's the whole playbook until I turn the bookmark into a habit.
