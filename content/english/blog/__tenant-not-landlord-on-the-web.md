"Why not just post on Facebook?" Glad I kept asking myself the other way around.

I get it every time I mention this site. Social is easy. The audience is already there. I still use Facebook for Messenger and a trimmed Favorites feed.

The metaphor that stuck for me isn't "own your platform" as a slogan. It's **tenant vs landlord**. On someone else's app, you're renting square footage. On your own domain, you're holding the deed to a small plot. For the full argument - cost, search, maintenance, what I actually keep here - see [why I still run my own website](/blog/why-run-your-own-website/). This post is the eviction notice I keep in my head.

---

## When the landlord tears the building down

On Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, you're a tenant. The landlord can change the lease, raise the rent, or demolish the block.

It's not hypothetical. Vine vanished overnight in 2017. Google+ shut down. MySpace lost over a decade of users' music. Flickr slashed free accounts to a thousand photos and wiped the rest. Facebook throttled organic reach for business pages from around 16% down to roughly 2%. TikTok keeps shifting under creators' feet. X broke thousands of tools when the API changed.

I felt the smaller version first - [shared PHP hosting died](/blog/how-i-built-jorap-notes/) and took files with it. Same lesson, personal scale. Rented land is rented land whether it's a feed or a cPanel login.

Your website doesn't run on someone else's pivot schedule. You decide what stays. If hosting fails, you point the domain elsewhere.

---

## What I refuse to leave in the lobby

This domain is where my **notes garden**, my **extension-cord** post, and my **Alex Eala tracker** live together - none of which I'd hand to a platform that can throttle reach or change export rules overnight.

Algorithms resurface what they want resurfaced. A long essay on my domain stays findable. A Facebook thread about the same thing is buried by lunch.

Keeping an idea here forces a small honesty check: if I'm not willing to give it a home on my own domain, maybe it wasn't worth saying out loud.

---

## The container is part of the message

Every social profile looks like every other profile on that platform. Same grid, same fonts, same feed shape.

On jorap.com I picked **Hugoplate** because I already knew Tailwind - not because the demo was prettiest. Notes garden gets its own nav. Blog posts and garden notes don't share the same template. I turned off half the theme's demo pages and kept what I actually publish.

When someone lands here, they're in my layout - tennis stats next to Instant Pot recipes in an order I chose, not a row of squares that looks like everyone else's.

---

## Analytics without the performance theater

On my own site I see what's read, where people came from, how long they stayed. Not vanity metrics for a feed - signal for what to write next and what to stop wasting time on.

Social gives you a thin slice, only for content posted there. I use both. The site is where the long view lives.

---

## What I actually do

I post on social for reach. The site holds what I want findable in five years.

I'm not asking you to quit Facebook. I sleep better knowing the deed is in a repo I can clone. If the tenant metaphor lands, start with one post you'd miss if the app disappeared tomorrow - then put the real copy on your own plot.
